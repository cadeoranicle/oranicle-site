Clinical & Operational Interpretation

🟡 Point #1 — Constraint

Low-Volume Efficiency Zone

This reflects months where overall activity levels are lower, yet the organization remains operationally viable. Such patterns may occur during seasonal slowdowns, reduced patient intake, staffing transitions, or service-line contraction.

  • Lower total patient encounters
  • Fewer services delivered per patient
  • Slight compensation through higher reimbursement per claim

🔵 Point #2 — Freedom

High Utilization Activity

This reflects months with elevated service intensity relative to patient volume. Often associated with complex case mix, specialty concentration, chronic disease management, or high-acuity populations.

  • Higher services delivered per patient
  • Increased repeat encounters or follow-up visits
  • Concentrated revenue generation within specific departments

⚫ Point #3 — Void / Outlier

Atypical Operational Pattern

These are uncommon monthly operating patterns compared to peer institutions in the region. The relationship between patient volume and service intensity differs noticeably from typical norms.

  • Unusual service-to-patient ratios
  • Distinct reimbursement structure relative to peers
  • Possible structural shifts in service mix

🟢 Point #4 — Core

Typical Operating Pattern

This reflects the most common monthly operating pattern observed across hospitals and provider groups in the region. It represents routine care delivery under stable demand and reimbursement conditions.

  • Patient volume proportionate to service intensity
  • No extreme repeat utilization patterns
  • Payment per claim and per patient within expected regional norms
